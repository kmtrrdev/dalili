Bansko (Bulgaria: Банско) is a mountain and ski resort in Southwest Bulgaria, under the slopes of the Pirin Mountains. It is the largest ski resort in the Balkans.

Understand

 
 
 
 
 
Bansko lies under the north-eastern slopes of the Pirin Mountains, on the southern edge of the Razlog Valley created by the meeting point of three mountain ranges: Rila and Pirin to the west and the Rhodope Mountains to the east. The town's official altitude is  above sea level, and its permanent population is about 9,000 in 2020.

While Bansko has been a ski resort since the Communist era, a boom in international visitors developed in the 2000s. As a result, the place is heavily covered in hotels and other establishments catering to tourists; real estate speculation is another thriving business. Since the 2010s, the town has also attracted a lot of foreign "digital nomads", a group with less seasonal variation.

Peak season in Bansko is in winter starting approximately mid-December. In summer the altitude combined with its southern location give it a mild continental climate. 

See


In a nice day, with good visibility the view of the mountains is beautiful, especially if you take the lift to the ski tracks. You should get to the ski tracks at least once, even if you do not ski yourself.

* 
The Old Town. Bansko is one of the oldest settlements in the foothills of Pirin Mountain. You can stroll around the narrow cobbled streets and enjoy the charming old-fashioned ambience of the traditional stone buildings.

* 
House of Velyan. Built during the 18th century, the House of Velyan is a national cultural heritage. The building has impressive frescoes and wood-carved ceilings.

* 
House-museum “Neofit Rilski”. Neofit Rilski is a prominent figure in the Bulgarian Renaissance. He is the founder of the Bulgarian secular education and the first Bulgarian encyclopedist. His house from the 18th century is turned into a museum to show the traditional architecture of Bansko.

* 
Radonova house. Radonova house is now a historical and ethnographic museum. Built in the 19th century, the house hosts a rich exposition of materials following the development of Bansko from its origins to the liberation in 1912. The ethnographic exhibition shows the lifestyle of Bansko people during that period.

* 
Holy Trinity Church. A fine example of middle and late Bulgarian National Revival church architecture. Beautiful interior. Next door to the Neofit Rilski house.


Do


Skiing

Bansko is a good place for skiing in winter. It has 14 operating lifts. It's the biggest ski resort in the Balkans, but a relatively small resort by international standards; 3.5 days is plenty. The resort is mainly suited to beginners, with few black runs, and those more advanced will get bored quite quickly. Also, don't expect the runs to be groomed too often. Skiers and snowboarders could find BanskoSki useful.
It is also a good idea to avoid any English school holidays as there will be insane amounts of people everywhere (and a 3-hr wait to get up the mountain via gondola), as well as the skiing world cup as half of the runs will be closed for the weeks leading up to it.

There is a paid car park in town next to the lower gondola station.

The gondola up to the mountain officially runs from 08:30–17:00, however it often starts a bit earlier. It takes 25 minutes. Expect a 55–60 minute queue, whether you start queueing at 08:00 or 10:00. If you want to be on the first lift, start queuing at 07:30. If you have lessons at 09:00, you must join the queue by 07:50 at the absolute latest.

Mobile phone reception on the slopes is excellent.

You can rent ski gear at the town gondola station and get a discount at the lift ticket, though if you want to leave your skis there overnight there is an extra charge. A lot of ski rental shops can be found near the first station, where you can probably rent gear in better condition and get a good price if you book for more than 1 day. Full ski gear for 4 days costs about €70, or €100 for premium equipment, as of 2026. Most ski shops allow you to leave the gear at the shop overnight for free. Rental shops include Ski Mania, Inter Bansko, and Grizzlys.

Elite Ski School and Ulen Ski School get to skip the gondola queue. Prices as of 2026 start from €56 for group training, or private training from €105 for the first person, €59 for each additional person.

The weather in Bansko is colder than some Greek ski resorts, so warmer clothes and a full face hood is nice to have.

Most hotels offer a bus service to the ski lift station in the morning and afternoon. Try to get to the lift station not later than 09:00 to avoid long queues. It is also advisable to buy the tickets from the previous afternoon for the same reason. There are also minibuses to ferry people up the mountain in the morning which is included in the lift pass.

Gondola tickets

You can get a multiday ticket for the lifts, although the discount is negligible. There is also a half day ticket after 12:30 or 13:00 each day, but you can also buy tickets at a better price from people leaving the ski centre before the end of the day, around the ticket offices.

There is a cheaper gondola ticket (about half price) if you are not skiing.  There are discounted tickets for children and teenagers.  There are several different ticket booths (labelled 'Kaca,' pronounced 'Casa'), so if one line is long, go to another ticket booth (including booth that is behind the main line of skiers entering the gondola).  Children's tickets require a photograph, so either have the child with you when purchasing ticket, or have a photograph on the phone.

Hiking

 
Bansko is an excellent entry way to the Pirin Mountains, Bulgaria's wildest and least developed mountain range. Vihren hut, named after the nearby Mt. Vihren, is the best place to begin both short and long distance treks. During the peak season (mid-Jun to mid-Sep) a bus will take you several times a day to and from Bansko. 

Great one day hikes include Mt. Vihren, the 2nd highest mountain in Bulgaria and 3rd highest in the Balkans, and The Koncheto, a 200-m-long and 1-m-wide ridge between the second and 3rd highest mountains in Pirin with vast cliffs on either side. With a full day it is possible to hike them both.

The highest peak of Pirin Mountain (Vihren, 2,916 m) is at a day's walk from the city.

Children/family activities

There are snow hills under the bottom of the gondola area.  No ticket is required and you do not need to go on the gondola.  Just walk there.  Watch out for the skiers (and the other sledders and tubers).  Children (and adults!) can sled, slide down the hills, and play on a playground. There is no control on the snow hills.  Sled rental is available at most of the ski rental shops.  Sleds should be €10 per day  Dec 2024). Go elsewhere or negotiate if asked for more.  Many of the shops on Pirin Street sell plastic toboggans (€15) and plastic sleds (€25 - €100 depending on quality).    

Magic Show at Queen's Pub at 20:00 (Jan 2025, may be only during the ski season - exact days unknown).  Sign board in front says 19:30 pm but show did not start until 20:00.  No charge.    

Ice skating on Pirin Street from around 12:00 noon to 9:00 pm.  €25 adults, €15 children  Sauna and steam bath, nice garden in summer with a broken jacuzzi. Walking distance from downtown and the ski gondola. Home made products on the buffet.

* 
Guinness Hotel. Full service hotel with options for breakfast and breakfast/dinner (half board). Large hotel with indoor atrium, indoor pool, children's room, bar. Walking distance to gondola and regular shuttle.

Premier Luxury Mountain Resort. A 5-star hotel. As a hotel guest you get free access to the spa and pool as well as the ski shuttle to the gondala every 30 min. There is free Wi-Fi in all public areas and all the rooms. Breakfast is included.

Explore Bansko. Chalets sleeping 16–21 people, catered or non-catered. Can arrange ski rental.
